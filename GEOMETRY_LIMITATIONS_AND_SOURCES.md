### 1. Educational Reconstruction Declaration

This 3D reconstruction of **Bab Zuwayla (Cairo, Egypt)** is designated as an **Approximate Educational Reconstruction** produced specifically for the documentary series *RUWAQ*.

In accordance with professional historical documentary standards and archaeological ethics:
- **No speculative numerical measurements are displayed.**
- **Arbitrary metric elevation callouts (+8.6m, +18.0m, +40.2m) and unverified metric scale bars (0–20m) have been removed from the visualizer.**
- Geometric proportions are preserved for historical demonstration, but the entire model is clearly identified as a proportional educational visualization pending future systematic terrestrial LiDAR and photogrammetric laser survey.

### 2. Historical & Archaeological Foundations

The monument embodies two distinct, monumental architectural campaigns spanning 329 years:

#### Phase I: The Fatimid Stone Gate (1092 CE / 485 AH)
- **Patron**: Badr al-Jamali (*Amir al-Juyush* / Commander of the Armies for Fatimid Caliph al-Mustansir Billah).
- **Architects**: Master Christian Armenian stone masons from Urfa/Edessa.
- **Architectural Signatures**:
  - Twin semicircular bastions built from massive dressed Mokattam limestone ashlar blocks.
  - Splayed arrow slits (*marami al-siham*) designed to cover the southern approach against Seljuk and nomadic incursions.
  - Semicircular plan designed to deflect heavy siege catapult stone projectiles and prevent direct line-of-fire ramming.
  - Slightly pointed entrance arch constructed with **joggled cushion voussoirs** (*al-sanjat al-wisadiyyah*), an iconic Fatimid architectural hallmark.
  - Spanning upper machicolation arch (*saqqatah*) bridging between the bastions to pour burning oil, quicklime, or projectiles upon breaching parties.
  - Colossal oak door leaves encased completely in horizontal wrought iron straps and studded with hundreds of hemispherical forged iron rivets, pivoting on massive stone socket pintles.

#### Phase II: The Mamluk Twin Minarets (1415–1421 CE / 818–824 AH)
- **Patron**: Sultan al-Mu'ayyad Abu al-Nasr Shaykh (Burji Mamluk Dynasty).
- **Master Architect**: Muhammad ibn al-Qazzaz (*Mu'allim al-Mi'mariyya*).
- **Architectural Signatures**:
  - Master stroke of urban adaptation: Erecting the twin minarets of the newly founded Mosque of Sultan al-Mu'ayyad directly on top of the solid stone roofs of the 323-year-old Fatimid gate towers.
  - **Six Architectural Levels**:
    1. Square-to-octagonal transition plinth with blind keel-arch niches.
    2. Lower corbelled balcony supported on a triple tier of carved stone *muqarnas* (stalactite) brackets.
    3. Slender octagonal middle shaft carved with intricate interlocking diagonal chevron/zigzag masonry relief (*al-zakhrafah al-zajzajiyyah*).
    4. Upper corbelled balcony with stone balustrade.
    5. Open octagonal colonnaded pavilion (*Jawsaq*) supported on eight slender stone colonnettes with horseshoe arches.
    6. Bulbous pear-shaped dome (*Mabkhara* / perfume-burner profile) crowned by bronze spire rings and an Islamic crescent moon (*Hilal*).

### 4. Known Geometrical Simplifications & Boundaries

To preserve real-time interactivity and deterministic 60fps / Remotion render speeds without inventing undocumented details:
1. **Interior Intrados Chambers**: Internal guardrooms, winding passages, and murder-hole chute cavities inside the solid bastion cores are omitted; the bastions are rendered as solid dressed stone masses.
2. **Spiral Staircases (*Hallazun*)**: Internal helical stone steps ascending within the towers and minaret shafts are simplified to exterior shafts.
3. **Subterranean Stratum**: The approximately 2 to 3 meters of original Fatimid street deposit accumulated since 1092 CE is normalized to the modern al-Mu'izz street level datum.
4. **Independent Manipulation Guarantee**: Every structural element retains an isolated coordinate space and pivot center so documentary animators can independently adjust camera angles, lateral bastion explosion, door swing, or vertical minaret separation without breaking adjacent assemblies.
