### 2. Historical & Architectural Campaigns

#### Campaign I: The Fatimid Fortified Stone Gate (1092 CE / 485 AH)
- **Patron**: Badr al-Jamali (*Amir al-Juyush* / Commander of the Armies for Fatimid Caliph al-Mustansir Billah).
- **Master Builders**: Armenian stone masons from Urfa/Edessa (known in Arabic sources as John, Mary, and Sarkis).
- **Key Architectural Features Preserved in Model**:
  - Twin semicircular bastions built from massive dressed Mokattam limestone ashlar blocks. The semicircular exterior curvature deflected catapult projectiles and allowed flanking enfilade fire against siege troops.
  - Splayed arrow slits (*marami al-siham*) positioned at two strategic defensive heights with deep interior reveals.
  - Slightly pointed entrance arch constructed with **joggled cushion voussoirs** (*al-sanjat al-wisadiyyah*), an iconic Fatimid architectural hallmark introduced to Egypt in 1087–1092 CE.
  - Spanning upper machicolation bridge arch (*saqqatah*) bridging between the bastions to pour boiling oil or quicklime upon attackers breaching the outer portal.
  - Continuous 11.4m vaulted passage running through the fortification into al-Mu'izz street, secured by colossal oak door leaves encased in wrought iron.

#### Campaign II: The Mamluk Twin Minarets (1415–1421 CE / 818–824 AH)
- **Patron**: Sultan al-Mu'ayyad Abu al-Nasr Shaykh (Burji Mamluk Dynasty).
- **Master Architect**: Muhammad ibn al-Qazzaz (*Mu'allim al-Mi'mariyya*).
- **Key Architectural Features Preserved in Model**:
  - Twin minarets erected directly upon the solid stone roofs of the 323-year-old Fatimid gate towers to serve the adjacent Mosque of Sultan al-Mu'ayyad.
  - Six distinct architectural levels:
    1. Square base plinth transitioning through squinches into an octagonal shaft adorned with blind keel-arch niches.
    2. Lower corbelled balcony supported on a triple tier of stone *muqarnas* (stalactite) corbels.
    3. Slender octagonal middle shaft carved with interlocking diagonal chevron/zigzag masonry relief (*al-zakhrafah al-zajzajiyyah*).
    4. Upper corbelled balcony with stone balustrade.
    5. Open octagonal colonnaded pavilion (*Jawsaq*) supported on eight slender stone colonnettes with horseshoe arches.
    6. Bulbous pear-shaped dome (*Mabkhara* / perfume-burner profile) crowned by bronze spire rings and an Islamic crescent moon (*Hilal*).

### 3. Documented Boundaries & Proportional Assumptions

In accordance with archaeological reconstruction ethics:
- **No Speculative Measurements**: Proportions are derived from Creswell's survey and high-resolution photogrammetric alignment without displaying speculative modern metric callouts.
- **Physical Passage Continuity**: The gate passage is modeled as an authentic, open 11.4m deep vaulted corridor, with a horizontal architectural plan cut implemented at Y = 5.0m to expose the open passage and semicircular tower footprints in top orthographic plan.
- **Separate Phase Hierarchy**: The 1092 CE gate and the 1421 CE minarets remain strictly independent components, allowing viewers to see the monument in its pristine original Fatimid state without Mamluk additions, as well as in its fully assembled later state.
